# Supermarket Sales Analysis & Management Dashboard

**Tools:** Microsoft Excel | PivotTables | PivotCharts | Excel Formulas | Data Cleaning | Statistical Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

The Supermarket Sales Analysis project focuses on analyzing supermarket sales data to understand overall business performance, product category contributions, outlet performance, sales relationships, and statistical patterns.

The project involves cleaning and preparing raw supermarket sales data, performing exploratory data analysis, calculating key business metrics, and developing a consolidated, interactive Excel dashboard.

The final dashboard presents important business insights in a single worksheet, helping management review sales performance and make informed business decisions.

## 2. Business Context

Rick is working as a Data Analyst Intern at a supermarket company. The management wants to understand the company's sales performance across different product categories and store outlets.

However, the sales data is available only in a raw dataset, making it difficult to identify the best-performing products, compare outlet performance, and understand sales patterns.

This project addresses the problem by transforming raw sales data into meaningful business insights through Microsoft Excel.

## 3. Project Goal

Analyze the supermarket sales dataset in Excel and create an interactive dashboard presenting:

- Key business performance metrics.
- Category-wise sales contributions.
- Outlet performance comparisons.
- Product visibility and sales relationships.
- Statistical insights into sales performance.

## 4. Dataset Overview

The dataset contains **8,523 product records and 12 original columns**.

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| Item Fat Content | Fat content classification of products |
| Item Identifier | Unique product identification |
| Item Type | Product category |
| Outlet Establishment Year | Year the outlet was established |
| Outlet Identifier | Unique outlet identification |
| Outlet Location Type | Outlet location classification (Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3) |
| Outlet Size | Size classification of the outlet |
| Outlet Type | Type of supermarket or grocery outlet |
| Item Visibility | Visibility of products in the outlet |
| Item Weight | Weight of the product |
| Sales | Sales value generated by the product |
| Rating | Product rating |

## 5. Data Cleaning and Preparation

The raw dataset was reviewed and prepared before performing the analysis.

Key data preparation activities:

- Checked the dataset structure and data types.
- Standardized inconsistent Item Fat Content values.
- Converted `LF` and `low fat` into `Low Fat`.
- Converted `reg` into `Regular`.
- Checked for duplicate records.
- Identified missing Item Weight values and retained them.
- Identified zero Item Visibility values and retained them.
- Checked missing values in important analytical columns.
- Verified the consistency of the sales data.

## 6. Project Objectives and Analysis

### Objective 1: Overall Sales Performance

Analyzed the overall sales performance of the supermarket by calculating:

1. Total sales revenue generated by the supermarket.
2. Average sales value per product record.
3. Average visibility of products across the supermarket.

**Key Results:**

| KPI | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Sales | ₹1,201,681.49 |
| Average Sales | ₹140.99 |
| Average Item Visibility | 0.06613 |

### Objective 2: Product Category and Sales Comparison

Analyzed product category contributions and compared sales across outlet types.

Key questions addressed:

- What percentage of total sales is contributed by the top four product categories compared with the remaining categories?
- Which product categories generate the highest and lowest total sales?
- How do total sales compare across different outlet types?

**Key Results:**

- The top four product categories contribute approximately **50.60%** of total sales.
- Fruits and Vegetables and Snack Foods are among the leading categories by total sales.
- Supermarket Type1 generates the highest total sales among the four outlet types.

### Objective 3: Outlet Performance and Sales Relationships

Analyzed outlet-level performance and explored relationships between product characteristics and sales.

Key questions addressed:

- How do sales vary across outlets for Low Fat and Regular products?
- Is there a relationship between product visibility and sales, and what trend can be observed?
- How do sales contributions from different product categories vary across outlet types?

**Key Findings:**

- Low Fat products contribute approximately 64.60% of total sales.
- Regular products contribute approximately 35.40% of total sales.
- Supermarket Type1 generates the highest sales for both fat-content classifications.
- The scatter plot and linear trendline indicate virtually no linear relationship between Item Visibility and Sales.
- Category-wise sales contributions vary across outlet types.

### Objective 4: Statistical Analysis of Sales

Performed statistical analysis to understand the distribution and variation of sales values.

Key questions addressed:

- What are the average, median, minimum, and maximum sales values?
- How much variation exists in sales across product records?
- Which records have unusually high or low sales compared with most records?
- How does average sales performance differ across product categories, outlet types, and outlet locations?

**Statistical Results:**

## 9. Key Business Insights

Based on the analysis, the following insights were identified:

1. The supermarket generated approximately ₹1.20 million in total sales.
2. The top four product categories contribute approximately half of total sales.
3. Supermarket Type1 is the leading outlet type in terms of total sales revenue.
4. Low Fat products contribute a higher share of sales than Regular products.
5. Item Visibility has virtually no linear relationship with Sales in this dataset.
6. Average sales values are relatively similar across outlet types and outlet locations.
7. The IQR method identified no unusually high or low sales records.

## 10. Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following actions may help management:

- Maintain product availability in high-performing categories.
- Review the sales performance of lower-performing product categories.
- Study the operational practices of Supermarket Type1 outlets to understand their stronger total sales performance.
- Evaluate Low Fat and Regular product demand when planning inventory.
- Avoid relying on product visibility alone to predict sales; consider other factors such as product category, pricing, outlet type, and customer demand.
- Continue monitoring average sales and outlet performance across different locations.

These recommendations are based on the observed dataset and should be validated with additional business information before implementation.
